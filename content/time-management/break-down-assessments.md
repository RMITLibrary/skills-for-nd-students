	<p class="lead margin-top-zero">Task initiation can be challenging, especially when an assessment feels large, vague or overwhelming. Task chunking means breaking an assessment into smaller, more manageable tasks.<p>
	<p>You can do this manually, but planning tools can make the process easier. The three tools below support different stages of planning and work particularly well when used together.</p>

	<h2>Using the tools together</h2>
	<p>These three tools complement one another well.</p>
	<ul>
		<li><strong>All-Assignment Planner</strong> – shows the <em>big picture</em> of all your assignments together across the semester.</li>
		<li><strong>RMIT Assignment Planner</strong> – shows the <em>big picture</em> and timeline of each assignment.</li>
		<li><strong>Magic To‑Do</strong> – helps you break individual steps into <em>doable actions</em>.</li>
	</ul>
	<p>For example:</p>
	<ul>
		<li>Use the All-Assignment Planner to analyse and assess the order you will work on your assignments.</li>
		<li>Use the Assignment Planner to identify today’s focus (e.g., “Research sources”).</li>
		<li>Put that task into Magic To‑Do to generate small, actionable steps.</li>
		<li>Choose just <strong>one or two steps</strong> to work on now.</li>
	</ul>
	<h2>A final reminder</h2>
	<p>
	You don’t need to use these tools perfectly, or all the time. If something stops helping, you can change it, pause it, or move on to something else.
	</p>
	<p>
	Planning tools are supports, not tests of organisation or motivation.
	</p>
	<p>
	If you’d like help using these tools in a way that works for <em>you</em>, an <a href="https://www.rmit.edu.au/students/support-services/study-support/consultations">Academic Skills Advisor</a> can talk through strategies and adapt them to your needs.
	</p>
